The taekwondo bus stops outside the apartment at 3:10. It is yellow, it is loud, and it is the highlight of Raon's day.

In Korea most kids start around six, partly for the discipline and partly because the dojang bus solves the after-school problem.

Raon learned to bow, to count to ten in Korean at full volume, and to tie his own belt, which took a week and about four hundred attempts. The belt is white. It is already turning gray.
